\section{The Program}
%% ===================================================================

The primitive object is a kernel $W$ on a set $X$ together with the
\emph{bilinear} pairing $\langle f,g\rangle = \int_X fg$. From it:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{polynomials} $P_n$ with
        $\int_X P_m P_n\, W = h_n \delta_{mn}$.
        Positivity of $W$ is \textbf{not} required --- only nonvanishing
        of the Hankel determinants of the moments $\int x^n W$. Signed
        and complex kernels are admitted.
  \item an analytic \emph{split} $W = w_+ w_-$ and the dual bases
        $F_n = w_+ \hat P_n$, $G_n = w_- \hat P_n$, biorthonormal:
        $\int F_m G_n = \delta_{mn}$.
  \item the \emph{matching matrix}
        $c_{mk} = \int_X \hat P_m \hat P_k\, w_+ W$,
        expanding one basis in the polynomials.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{orthogonal} case is the self-dual specialisation: $w_+ = w_-
= \sqrt{W}$, $G_n = \overline{F_n} = \Phi_n$, and the pairing becomes
the $L^2$ inner product. Hermite (Gaussian) is exactly self-dual; MP
taught us the split $\Gamma(\tfrac14+ix)\cdot\Gamma(\tfrac14-ix)$ beats
the symmetric root $\sqrt{W}$ --- the arithmetic lives in the dual
pair, not the orthogonal functions. So the plan admits any kernel with
a good split, whether or not a positive orthogonal theory exists.

For each new kernel the questions are always: decay rate of $c_{0k}$,
integrality (over which ring), generating function, and zeros of the
truncated approximants.

\subsection*{What the first two instances taught}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & Hermite & Meixner--Pollaczek ($\lambda=\tfrac14$) \\
\midrule
kernel $W$      & $e^{-x^2}$ (entire) & $\Gamma(\tfrac14+ix)\Gamma(\tfrac14-ix)$ (poles at $\pm i\tfrac14$) \\
decay $b_{0k}$  & geometric $3^{-k/2}$ & power law $k^{-\alpha}$, $\alpha\approx\tfrac34$ \\
integrality     & $\Z$, directly & none for $\sqrt{W}$; dual pair $\Rightarrow \Z A \oplus \Z B$ \\
generators      & $1$ & $A,B = \sqrt{2e}\,K_0(\tfrac12),\,\sqrt{2e}\,K_1(\tfrac12)$ \\
GF              & $e^{(\text{quadratic})}$ & $e^{\zeta/2}K_0(\zeta/2)$, $\zeta$ = product of Cayley transforms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textbf{Moral: the family name is irrelevant; the analytic class of the
kernel decides everything.} The plan below is therefore organised by
strata of weight analyticity, with one canonical representative each.

%% ===================================================================
\section{The Strata}
%% ===================================================================

\subsection{Entire kernels \hfill \normalfont\emph{done}}

$W = e^{-x^2}$ on $\R$, Hermite $H_n$. Geometric decay, integer
matching matrix, closed GF. The base case.

\subsection{One pair of $\Gamma$'s \hfill \normalfont\emph{done ($\lambda=\tfrac14$)}}

$W = |\Gamma(\lambda+ix)|^2$ on $\R$, Meixner--Pollaczek
$P_n^{(\lambda)}(x;\tfrac\pi2)$. Bump--Ng: for $\lambda=\tfrac14$ the
functions are Mellin transforms of even Hermite functions --- the
$\Gamma(s/2)$ factor of $\xi$. Dual pair restores arithmetic; decay
stays slow (pole at finite distance).

\paragraph{2a. The other parameter, $\lambda=\tfrac34$.}
Same machinery, kernel $|\Gamma(\tfrac34+ix)|^2$ = Mellin side of
\emph{odd} Hermite functions = gamma factor $\Gamma(\frac{s+1}{2})$ of
odd Dirichlet $L$-functions. It has already knocked twice: the spurious
zeros of $F_0^{(N)}$ converge onto the line $\operatorname{Im} x =
-\tfrac12$ (the $\lambda=\tfrac34$ line), and the joint weight
collapses:
\[
  |\Gamma(\tfrac14+ix)|^2\,|\Gamma(\tfrac34+ix)|^2
  \;=\; \frac{2\pi^2}{\cosh 2\pi x}.
\]

\paragraph{2b. The joint $\tfrac14\otimes\tfrac34$ matrix.}
Cross matching coefficients
$\int \hat P^{(1/4)}_m \hat P^{(3/4)}_k\, \frac{dx}{\cosh 2\pi x}$
(and variants): an \emph{elementary} kernel, $\operatorname{sech}$ =
its own Fourier transform, poles at $\pm\tfrac{i}{4}$. Every integral
is cheap; this is the fastest route to a genuinely new table.
Expect: two-family checkerboard, and a chance of closed forms
(the $\operatorname{sech}$ moment integrals are classical --- Euler
numbers).

\subsection{Two pairs of $\Gamma$'s: continuous Hahn \hfill \normalfont\emph{next}}

$W = |\Gamma(a+ix)\Gamma(b+ix)|^2$ on $\R$, continuous Hahn
$p_n(x;a,b)$. These are the Mellin transforms of \emph{Laguerre}
functions --- the half-line theory pushed through Bump--Ng. Matching
integrals now have $3$ or $4$ whole $\Gamma$-pairs: genuine Barnes
integrals. The Kummer-$U$ lemma that cracked the MP dual table sits
one step \emph{below} Barnes' first lemma; here Barnes' lemmas apply
natively. Prediction: exact closed forms return, integrality over a
ring generated by hypergeometric constants at fixed argument.

\subsection{Four $\Gamma$'s: Wilson \hfill \normalfont\emph{after Hahn}}

$W = \bigl|\frac{\prod_{j} \Gamma(a_j+ix)}{\Gamma(2ix)}\bigr|^2$,
Wilson polynomials $W_n(x^2;a,b,c,d)$ --- top of the Askey scheme,
orthogonality \emph{is} a Barnes-type integral (de~Branges, Wilson).
Everything below is a degeneration; if a uniform statement about
matching matrices exists, it lives here. High risk, high yield;
do after continuous Hahn calibrates expectations.

\subsection{Half-line: Laguerre \hfill \normalfont\emph{control}}

$W = x^\alpha e^{-x}$ on $(0,\infty)$, Laguerre $L_n^{(\alpha)}$.
Kernel entire in the bulk but with a branch point at the endpoint $0$
(for $\alpha \notin 2\Z$). Interpolates the Hermite and Jacobi
behaviours: expect geometric decay from $e^{-x}$, corrections from the
endpoint. Also the Mellin \emph{preimage} of stratum 2.3 --- compute
both sides and watch the transform carry structure across.

\subsection{Compact interval: Jacobi \hfill \normalfont\emph{control}}

$W = (1-x)^\alpha(1+x)^\beta$ on $[-1,1]$, Jacobi $P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}$.
No $\xi$ connection; included as the pure-endpoint-singularity control.
Chebyshev ($\alpha=\beta=\mp\tfrac12$) is the degenerate corner where
$W^{3/2}$ is again a Chebyshev weight --- the matching matrix should be
computable in closed form by hand, a useful sanity anchor.

\subsection{Discrete kernels: Charlier, Meixner, Krawtchouk \hfill \normalfont\emph{control / combinatorial}}

$W(x) = e^{-a}a^x/x!$ on $\N$ (Charlier $C_n(x;a)$);
$\beta^{(x)}c^x/x!$ (Meixner); $\binom{N}{x}p^xq^{N-x}$ (Krawtchouk).
Matching matrices are \emph{sums}; integrality is automatic, so the
question inverts: which phenomena (checkerboard, phase law,
two-generator structure) survive, and which were artifacts of the
contour? Motivated from inside: the MP dual GF factors through a
\emph{Charlier kernel} $e^{\sigma+\tau-\sigma\tau}$
(meixner-pollaczek-simple.tex \S6) --- the discrete family is already
embedded in the continuous answer. The conjectured $2$-state automaton
($K_0 \leftrightarrow K_1$) should be \emph{provable} here first.

\subsection{Genuinely dual: no orthogonal counterpart \hfill \normalfont\emph{new territory}}

Once the pairing is bilinear, the kernel need not be symmetric,
positive, or even real. Three sub-cases, in increasing wildness:

\paragraph{7a. Unbalanced $\Gamma$ split.}
$W = \Gamma(a+ix)\,\Gamma(b-ix)$ with $a \ne b$. Not of the form
$|\cdot|^2$: no positive weight, no orthogonal functions --- but the
moments exist (the $3$-$\Gamma$ lemma \emph{is} the moment generating
identity, and it never needed $a=b$), the Hankel determinants can be
checked, and the whole dual machinery runs verbatim. The natural first
instance: $a=\tfrac14$, $b=\tfrac34$ --- pairing the $\Gamma(s/2)$ and
$\Gamma(\frac{s+1}{2})$ worlds inside one bilinear form, the dual-basis
shadow of the joint $\operatorname{sech}$ matrix of 2b.

\paragraph{7b. Classical biorthogonal systems.}
Systems born biorthogonal: Konhauser pairs (polynomials in $x$ against
polynomials in $x^k$, Laguerre weight); Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials
(kernel $\frac{1}{x+y}$, two measures); Laurent biorthogonal
polynomials (Toda / continued fractions); Wilson's biorthogonal
\emph{rational} functions ($\mathstrut_{9}F_8$ level, above the Askey
scheme). These test whether our phenomena need the polynomial--times--%
split form at all, or survive in genuinely two-variable pairings.

\paragraph{7c. $\zeta$-adapted kernels.}
The endgame. Nothing now forbids kernels built from the object of
interest itself: the zeta-basis pairing
$\langle F_1,F_2\rangle_\zeta = \int F_1 \overline{F_2}\,|\zeta|^{-2}$
already lives here, and one can attempt splits
$W = \xi(\tfrac12+ix)\cdot(\cdots)$ where positivity fails on every
zero but Hankel nonvanishing may hold. Dual bases are the only
framework in which such kernels are legal.

\subsection{$q$-kernels \hfill \normalfont\emph{horizon}}

Askey--Wilson $|(e^{2i\theta};q)_\infty / \prod_j (a_j e^{i\theta};q)_\infty|^2$.
Natural boundary at $|q|=1$ --- a genuinely new analytic class (kernel
not continuable at all). Park here until the classical column is done;
coordinate with Rick's Hopf-algebra work if it comes alive.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Order of work.}
(1) $\lambda=\tfrac34$ and the joint $\operatorname{sech}$ matrix ---
verbatim port of existing code, elementary integrals, new arithmetic
likely.
(2) The unbalanced split $\Gamma(\tfrac14+ix)\Gamma(\tfrac34-ix)$ ---
first kernel with \emph{no} orthogonal theory; the $3$-$\Gamma$ lemma
already covers its moments.
(3) Continuous Hahn --- does Barnes restore closed forms?
(4) Charlier --- prove the $2$-state combinatorial model where sums
replace contours.
Then Laguerre/Wilson, Jacobi--Chebyshev as the by-hand anchor, the
born-biorthogonal systems, and finally $\xi$-built kernels.
